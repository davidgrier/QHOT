Traps Tab

The Traps tab lists the traps in a spreadsheet. There is a row for each trap, and the rows follow the traps as you move them on the screen. Edit a value by clicking on it, typing a number and pressing Return. Point at a value and the status bar says which property of which trap it is.

A single trap has these properties:

  • x, y: the position in the image [pixels]
  • z: the position along the optical axis [pixels]; a trap is in focus at the value of zc on the CGH tab
  • A: the relative amplitude of the trap
  • φ: the relative phase of the trap [radians]

Kinds of trap

Add a trap of any kind from the Tasks menu, in Add Trap. It appears on the optical axis. Traps with structure have more properties, which follow the first ones in the row. Point at one for its name.

QTweezer
A single focused spot.
QVortex
A ring of light with a twist. ell is its topological charge: the number of times the phase winds around the ring.
QRingTrap
A ring of light. radius is the radius of the ring [pixels], and ell is its topological charge.
QTrapArray
A rectangular grid of tweezers. nx and ny are the numbers of columns and rows, separation is the spacing between neighbors [pixels], and fuzz is the size of a random displacement of each tweezer [pixels].
QLetterArray
A letter drawn in dots, one tweezer for each dot, on a grid of 5 columns and 7 rows. It begins as the letter A. separation is the spacing between dots [pixels].
QTextArray
A word of letter arrays side by side. It begins as the word NYU. separation is the spacing between dots [pixels].

Groups

The traps in a group move together, and a group has a row of its own that gives the position of its center. A rectangular array, a letter or a word is a group; so is a set of traps that you gather. The traps of a group are listed below it, indented. A group has no amplitude or phase of its own: the amplitude and phase of each member are its own. Move a group by changing the position in its row, and the whole group follows.
