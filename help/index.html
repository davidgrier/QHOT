QHOT User Manual

QHOT is a point-and-click interface for holographic optical trapping and video microscopy. A spatial light modulator (SLM) projects optical traps into the field of view of the microscope, and the camera shows the traps and the particles that they hold.

The controls are grouped into tabs in the bar above this browser. Click a tab to use it. This browser is in the Help tab: its Back, Forward and Home buttons move through the pages.

The tabs

  • Video: the recorder, the camera and the display filters.
  • Traps: the position, amplitude and phase of each trap.
  • Tasks: a queue of steps that QHOT carries out for you.
  • CGH: the calibration of the optical train.
  • SLM: a preview of the hologram.
  • Help: this manual.

Starting QHOT

Run qhot from a terminal. Flags on the command line choose the camera, the hologram backend and the SLM window:

-g a GenICam camera through viva-genicam
-f -b -i -m -v a GenICam camera through the producer of its maker: FLIR, Basler, IDS, MATRIX VISION, Allied Vision
-can OpenCV camera (a webcam)
-pa Raspberry Pi camera
-t -j -n -u the hologram backend: PyTorch, numba, NumPy, CuPy
-s show the SLM in a window on the main screen, even if the computer has a second screen
--log-level LEVEL show the messages that QHOT and QVideo log, at this level and above: DEBUG, INFO, WARNING, ERROR or CRITICAL. Use DEBUG to see why a camera will not open.
--version say which versions of QHOT and QVideo are installed, and stop

A number after the flag chooses among several cameras of the same kind. With no camera flag QHOT shows a simulated camera. With no backend flag it computes holograms with PyTorch if that is installed, then numba, then NumPy. qhot -h lists every flag.
